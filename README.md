# Curriculum & EIKEN Alignment Analysis

## Overview

A two-month curriculum research and analysis project examining the relationship between an existing English-language curriculum and external EIKEN proficiency requirements.

The project combined manual examination analysis, structured data collection, curriculum annotation and evidence-based curriculum development.

The work was conducted from the perspective of both an English graduate and an experienced classroom practitioner, with 14 years of experience teaching and developing English curriculum in Japan.

> **Confidentiality note:** The original project involved proprietary curriculum materials. This public repository intentionally excludes textbook titles, page references, proprietary examples, internal documents and company-specific curriculum data. The examples and structures presented here demonstrate the methodology rather than reproduce the original materials.

---

## Project Objectives

The project had three main objectives:

1. **Analyse curriculum alignment with EIKEN levels**
2. **Develop additional support for teachers and learners**
3. **Propose a framework for future curriculum development**

---

## 1. External Assessment Analysis

The first stage involved manually analysing EIKEN examination material to identify:

* Grammar structures
* Vocabulary and language patterns
* Question types
* Reading and listening demands
* Functional language
* Sentence structures
* Differences in receptive and productive difficulty

The examination material was reviewed manually rather than classified using AI.

The purpose was not simply to create a list of grammar points, but to develop a practical understanding of what language demands were actually represented at different proficiency levels.

The resulting evidence was organised into a structured Notion database.

### Example classification framework

Each observation could be considered as:

**Direct correlation**

The same or substantially equivalent language structure appears in both the external assessment evidence and the curriculum material.

**Related correlation**

The curriculum contains a related structure or language function, but the relationship is not sufficiently exact to describe it as a direct match.

**Level/context correlation**

The individual language item is not directly evidenced, but its complexity, context or combination with other structures provides useful evidence about approximate proficiency level.

**No direct evidence**

The curriculum feature could not be supported by the examined assessment evidence.

This distinction was important because it prevented weak or superficial matches from being presented as exact equivalences.

---

## 2. Curriculum Annotation & Data Structuring

The next stage involved manually reviewing curriculum materials and recording relevant language features.

A structured dataset was developed to capture areas such as:

* Grammar structures
* Vocabulary
* Language functions
* Reading demands
* Writing demands
* Listening/assessment-format exposure
* Potential difficulty
* Possible external proficiency alignment
* Areas requiring further evidence

The physical curriculum materials were also manually annotated during the review process.

The resulting analysis was then cross-referenced against the external assessment database.

This created a workflow from:

**Assessment evidence → structured database → curriculum annotation → cross-reference → written analysis**

---

## 3. Evidence-Based Analysis

A central feature of the project was distinguishing between **language complexity** and **task difficulty**.

For example, recognising a grammatical structure and independently producing that structure are different demands.

Similarly, an individual grammar point may appear at one proficiency level while a longer sentence combines several structures and creates a substantially greater processing or production demand.

The analysis therefore considered:

* Recognition vs. production
* Individual grammar vs. combined structures
* Vocabulary vs. grammatical complexity
* Sentence-level vs. connected-text processing
* Reading vs. listening demands
* Receptive vs. productive tasks

This helped move the analysis beyond simply asking:

> "Is this grammar point present?"

and toward:

> "What does the learner actually have to do with this language?"

---

## 4. From Analysis to Curriculum Support

The research was also used to identify practical ways of supporting teachers, learners and families.

Proposals included:

* Explicit identification of new grammar and vocabulary
* Visual representations of language concepts
* Mnemonic and memory strategies
* Teacher-ready diagrams and explanations
* Role-play and guided language practice
* Mapping classroom activities to specific language objectives
* Additional writing scaffolding
* Clearer progression between curriculum levels

The underlying principle was to reduce unnecessary cognitive and preparation load for teachers while making difficult language more visible to learners.

---

## 5. Curriculum Development Framework

The final stage translated the research into proposals for future curriculum development.

The proposed process was:

```text
Define target proficiency
        ↓
Identify required language skills
        ↓
Map language across curriculum levels
        ↓
Identify gaps and overlaps
        ↓
Develop supporting materials
        ↓
Test with learners and teachers
        ↓
Review evidence
        ↓
Refine curriculum
```

The project also identified the importance of maintaining alignment between classroom experience, teacher training and curriculum progression rather than treating each element independently.
